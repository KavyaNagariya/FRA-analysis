A cutaway transformer core and disc winding specimen excited by sweep frequency generator. A tuned resonance carriage glides across calibrated guide rails to evaluate sub-band mechanical deviations against deterministic standards.

Full Apparatus View
Sub-Band I (<2 kHz) Core & Flux

Evaluates magnetic core laminations, ground straps, and core magnetizing inductance (Lm). Physical core shifts or residual DC magnetization produce low-frequency anti-resonance drifts.

Sub-Band II (2–100 kHz) Winding Mechanics

Direct distributed RLC network: series inductance (Ls), turn-to-turn capacitance (Cs), and shunt ground capacitance (Cg). Radial buckling and axial compression alter geometric spacing, shifting notch resonances.

Sub-Band III (>100 kHz) Leads & Bushings

Dominated by internal lead inductances (Llead) and bushing capacitive grading (C1, C2). Highly sensitive to turret connection looseness, tap changer lead orientation, and test lead earthing.

Rigorous foundations in apparatus physics.

Rather than treating diagnostics as a black-box machine learning classifier, FRA Intelligence grounds every finding in observable circuit parameters, resonance shifts, and international standards.

Distributed RLC Ladder

Every transformer winding acts as a distributed passive ladder of series inductance (Ls), mutual coupling (M), and disk capacitances (Cs, Cg). Mechanical shifts change geometry, which directly alters resonant frequencies.

IEEE Sub-Band Standards

Strict adherence to IEEE Std C57.149-2012 and IEC 60076-18 sub-band criteria isolates core displacement (<2 kHz) from winding axial/radial distortion (2 kHz – 100 kHz) and lead looseness (>100 kHz).

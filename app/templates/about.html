Section 01

Distributed Parameter RLC Ladder Network

In low-frequency power engineering, a transformer winding is conventionally treated as a lumped inductive element ($L$) with slight series resistance ($R$). However, under high-frequency excitation (20 Hz to 2 MHz), the geometric arrangement of the disk coils, turns, insulation barriers, and tank enclosure behaves as a complex, multi-resonant distributed parameter ladder network.

Because physical dimensions dictate localized capacitance ($C = \varepsilon A / d$) and inductance ($L = \mu N^2 A / l$), any physical displacement caused by through-fault electromagnetic forces or shipping impacts permanently shifts the transfer function:

Distributed Ladder Representation of Winding Disk Section
Series Capacitance ($C_s$) Inter-disk and inter-turn insulation geometry. Changes during axial winding compression, disk tilting, or turn telescoping.
Shunt Capacitance ($C_g$) Radial distance between winding and core leg or grounded tank wall. Altered during radial hoop buckling or coil bulging.
Mutual Inductance ($M$) Magnetic coupling between physical disk coils. Governed by magnetic core permeability and coil alignment.
Section 02

IEEE C57.149 Standard Sub-Band Partitioning

IEEE Std C57.149 partitions the complete 2 MHz spectrum into three distinct physical diagnostic bands. Each band corresponds to a different dominant circuit regime within the apparatus:

Sub-Band 1 < 2 kHz

Core & Magnetization

Governed primarily by the main core leg open-circuit magnetizing inductance ($L_m$). Parallel resonance zero occurs at the first notch ($f_0$).

• Core sheet displacement
• Grounding strap discontinuity
• Residual remanence flux
Sub-Band 2 2 kHz – 100 kHz

Winding Structure

Governed by leakage inductance and disk capacitances ($L_s, C_s, C_g$). Contains multiple poles and zeros sensitive to millimeter-level coil shifts.

• Axial coil displacement
• Radial hoop buckling
• Turn-to-turn insulation breakdown
Sub-Band 3 > 100 kHz

Leads & Bushings

Governed by internal connection leads, turret geometries, bushing capacitances ($C_1, C_2$), and tap changer wiring.

• Bushing tap lead looseness
• DETC / OLTC switch misalignment
• Test lead grounding errors
Section 03

Mathematical Evaluation Formulations

1. Pearson Cross-Correlation Coefficient ($\rho_{xy}$ / $R^2$)

Evaluates spectral similarity between baseline $X$ and current sweep $Y$ across $N$ frequency points:

\(\rho_{xy} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \bar{X})^2 \sum_{i=1}^N (Y_i - \bar{Y})^2}}\)
Threshold: $\rho \ge 0.95$ Normal · $0.85 \le \rho < 0.95$ Advisory · $\rho < 0.85$ Severe

2. Absolute Sum of Logarithmic Error (ASLE)

Measures decibel deviation across logarithmic frequency intervals:

\(\text{ASLE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |20\log_{10}(Y_i) - 20\log_{10}(X_i)|\)
Threshold: $\text{ASLE} < 1.0\text{ dB}$ Normal · $\text{ASLE} > 3.0\text{ dB}$ Anomaly

SECTION 01 |

Physical Principles & Distributed RLC Circuit Model

IEEE Std C57.149 Clause 4.2

Sweep Frequency Response Analysis (SFRA) evaluates power transformer mechanical integrity by treating each winding as a passive, distributed parameter linear electrical network. Unlike low-frequency impedance measurements, high-frequency signals experience the winding not as a lumped coil, but as a complex ladder of series and mutual inductances coupled with distributed series and ground capacitances.

Because physical dimensions—such as coil disk spacing, distance to core legs, radial distance to outer windings, and tank clearance—dictate the capacitive and inductive field geometry, any mechanical deformation directly alters the localized circuit parameters:

Inductive Topology ($L_s, M, L_m$)
Determined by magnetic core geometry, iron permeability ($\mu_r$), turn count, and mutual coupling ($M_{ij}$) between adjacent disk sections. Shifts low-to-mid frequency resonance zeros.
Capacitive Topology ($C_s, C_g, C_b$)
Determined by physical insulation thickness, oil permittivity ($\varepsilon_r$), disk-to-disk axial clearance ($C_s$), and winding-to-ground radial distance ($C_g$). Governs high-frequency resonance poles.
TRANSFER FUNCTION FORMULATION: IEEE C57.149 EQ (1)
$$H(j\omega) = \frac{V_{\text{response}}(j\omega)}{V_{\text{source}}(j\omega)} = \frac{Z_{\text{load}}}{Z_{\text{winding}}(j\omega) + Z_{\text{load}}}$$
Magnitude Spectrum: $20 \log_{10} |H(j\omega)| \text{ [dB]}$ Phase Spectrum: $\angle H(j\omega) = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}(H)}{\text{Re}(H)}\right) \text{ [deg]}$
WINDING RLC EQUIVALENT LADDER DISTRIBUTED MODEL
• $L_s, M_{ij}$ Series & Mutual Inductance: Winding turns & core permeability
• $C_s$ Series Inter-disk Capacitance: Axial disk spacing & spacer geometry
• $C_g$ Shunt Ground Capacitance: Radial distance to core leg & tank
SECTION 02 |

IEEE Std C57.149 & IEC 60076-18 Sub-band Decomposition

Frequency Sub-band Partitioning

Because different physical transformer structures dominate the electrical response within distinct frequency regimes, both IEEE Std C57.149-2012 and IEC 60076-18 define three standardized diagnostic sub-bands. Decomposing the sweep into these sub-bands allows localized mechanical fault isolation without masking across the spectrum:

SUB-BAND 1 < 2 kHz

Core Magnetizing & Inductive Region

In this low-frequency band, capacitive impedances ($1/\omega C$) are exceedingly high (open circuits). The response is strictly governed by the magnetic core geometry, iron core lamination permeability, and bulk winding resistance.

PHYSICAL GOVERNING ELEMENTS:
Core Magnetizing Inductance ($L_m$), Core Residual Flux
TARGET FAULT DETECTIONS:
• Main Core Displacement or Shifting
• Core Lamination Short-Circuits
• Open or Unintended Multiple Core Grounds
• Residual DC Magnetization
Threshold Target: R² ≥ 0.95
SUB-BAND 2 2 kHz — 100 kHz

Winding Structure & Resonance Region

The core magnetically decouples as leakage flux dominates. The curve exhibits complex resonance peaks and anti-resonance troughs formed by the interaction between winding leakage inductance ($L_\sigma$) and inter-disk capacitance ($C_s$).

PHYSICAL GOVERNING ELEMENTS:
Winding Inductance ($L_\sigma$), Inter-disk ($C_s$) & Ground ($C_g$)
TARGET FAULT DETECTIONS:
• Radial Winding Buckling (Hoop Stress)
• Axial Winding Displacement / Tilting
• Coil Clamping Loss / Spacer Loosening
• Inter-turn Short-Circuit Faults
Threshold Target: R² ≥ 0.95, Δf < 2%
SUB-BAND 3 > 100 kHz

Terminal Leads, Bushings & Contacts

At frequencies above 100 kHz (up to 2 MHz), winding inductances present extremely high impedance. The response is governed primarily by terminal leads, bushing internal capacitances, tap-changer leads, and test grounding braid inductance.

PHYSICAL GOVERNING ELEMENTS:
Lead Inductance, Bushing Capacitance ($C_b$), Ground Braids
TARGET FAULT DETECTIONS:
• Tap Changer Selector Contact Anomaly
• Loose Internal Terminal Connections
• Bushing Shield & Insulation Degradation
• Test Lead Grounding Artifact Errors
Threshold Target: R² ≥ 0.90
SECTION 03 |

Mathematical Correlation & Analytical Evaluation Formulations

IEEE Std C57.149 Annex B / DL/T 911
1. CROSS-CORRELATION FACTOR (CCF / R²) DL/T 911 & IEEE C57.149

The normalized cross-correlation coefficient evaluates spectral shape similarity between the reference baseline vector ($X_i$) and the current test sweep ($Y_i$) across discrete frequency samples ($N$).

$$\text{CCF}(X, Y) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^N (X_i - \bar{X})^2 \cdot \sum_{i=1}^N (Y_i - \bar{Y})^2}}$$
IEEE C57.149 / DL/T 911 EVALUATION THRESHOLDS:
R² ≥ 0.95 Nominal / Good Agreement (Normal Condition)
0.85 ≤ R² < 0.95 Slight Deviation / Advisory (Warrant Investigation)
R² < 0.85 Severe Deviation / Critical Alarm (Deformation Suspected)
2. ABSOLUTE SUM OF LOGARITHMIC ERROR (ASLE) IEEE C57.149 Clause B.3

While CCF evaluates curve pattern shape, ASLE quantifies vertical amplitude offsets and damping shifts, avoiding correlation bias when curve shapes align but absolute magnitude levels have shifted.

$$\text{ASLE}(X, Y) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left| 20\log_{10}(X_i) - 20\log_{10}(Y_i) \right|$$
ASLE METRIC INTERPRETATION THRESHOLDS:
ASLE < 0.6 dB Excellent agreement, zero mechanical variation
0.6 dB ≤ ASLE < 1.5 dB Moderate deviation, insulation/aging or mild shift
ASLE ≥ 1.5 dB Substantial magnitude offset, mechanical change
3. PEAK RESIDUAL DEVIATION (|ΔdB_max|) IEEE C57.149 Clause 6.1

Identifies the singular maximum decibel difference between baseline and test response across a specific sub-band $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$. Pinpoints sharp resonant peak shifts.

$$|\Delta\text{dB}_{\max}| = \max_{f \in [f_{\min}, f_{\max}]} \left| X_{\text{test}}(f) - X_{\text{baseline}}(f) \right|$$
• Nominal: $|\Delta\text{dB}| < 2.5\text{ dB}$
• Warning: $2.5\text{ dB} \le |\Delta\text{dB}| < 6.0\text{ dB}$
• Critical: $|\Delta\text{dB}| \ge 6.0\text{ dB}$ (localized resonance alteration)
4. RESONANCE FREQUENCY SHIFT RATIO CIGRE Brochure 342

Measures relative percentage drift of identifiable resonance poles $f_{\text{res}} = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{L_{\text{eff}}C_{\text{eff}}})$. An increase in capacitance shifts poles downward; inductance loss shifts poles upward.

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f_0} = \frac{\left| f_{\text{test, peak}} - f_{\text{baseline, peak}} \right|}{f_{\text{baseline, peak}}} \times 100\%$$
• Stable: $\Delta f / f_0 < 1.0\%$ (thermal/oil variation)
• Advisory: $1.0\% \le \Delta f / f_0 < 3.0\%$ (slight physical shift)
• Alert: $\Delta f / f_0 \ge 3.0\%$ (significant geometry displacement)

Transformer Failure Mechanisms & SFRA Diagnostic Signature Matrix

Field Diagnostics Matrix

Correlating observed spectral deviations to specific transformer failure modes based on IEEE Std C57.149 Clause 6 and CIGRE WG A2.26 field recommendations:

Failure Mechanism Dominant Band Characteristic SFRA Curve Manifestation Field Verification Protocol
Radial Winding Deformation (Buckling) 2 kHz – 100 kHz Resonance peaks and anti-resonance troughs in the mid-frequency band shift toward lower frequencies due to increased winding-to-ground capacitance ($C_g$). New distinct resonance ripples appear. Perform low-voltage leakage reactance (LVLR) test; compare per-phase values (>1% shift indicates radial deformation).
Axial Winding Displacement / Tilting 2 kHz – 100 kHz Alteration in inter-disk series capacitance ($C_s$) and mutual inductance ($M$). Resonance poles shift upward or damp out. Marked asymmetry between Phase A and Phase C sweeps. Perform excitation current test; verify mechanical tie-rod clamping torque and bushing alignment.
Core Lamination Movement / Shifting < 2 kHz Low-frequency antiresonance notch shifts significantly along frequency axis ($>5\%$). Inductive curve slope between 50 Hz and 1 kHz changes angle due to altered reluctance path. Execute single-phase excitation current at low voltage; inspect core grounding current telemetry.
Unintended Core Multiple Grounds < 2 kHz Circulating eddy current loops through multiple ground straps introduce parasitic damping, causing flattening of the initial inductive slope and attenuation of the first resonance peak. Disconnect external core ground link; measure core-to-tank DC insulation resistance with 1000V Megohmmeter ($>100\text{ M}\Omega$).
Inter-Turn Insulation Short-Circuit All Sub-bands Massive upward shift across the low-frequency inductive curve (>15 dB), completely eliminating the primary antiresonance notch. Mid-frequency poles shift drastically upward. Immediate DGA oil test for acetylene ($C_2H_2$) and ethylene; perform transformer turns ratio (TTR) and DC resistance.
Tap Changer Lead Anomaly / Contact > 100 kHz Localized resonance variations beyond 100 kHz. Severe attenuation or anomalous anti-resonance notch appearing exclusively on specific tap selector positions. Perform dynamic resistance measurement (DRM) across all tap positions (1 through 33) to identify contact wear.
Test Ground Lead Artifact (Measurement Error) > 300 kHz Divergence appearing strictly at high frequencies (> 300 kHz to 2 MHz) caused by excessive ground braid length or poor grounding contact onto the bushing flange. Re-verify flat braided grounding strap length (< 100mm) and ensure metallic contact with unpainted bushing flange.
SECTION 05 |

Standardized Test Configurations & Field Grounding Protocols

IEC 60076-18 Clause 5 & 6
1. End-to-End Open Circuit

Source applied to one winding terminal (e.g. H1), measurement taken at opposite terminal of same phase (H0). All other windings floating. Exposes core and winding series parameters.

2. End-to-End Short Circuit

Secondary winding terminals short-circuited together. Eliminates core magnetizing inductance, directly isolating leakage inductance and isolating winding mechanical distortion.

3. Inter-Winding Capacitive

Signal injected into HV winding terminal; response measured on corresponding LV winding terminal. Highly sensitive to inter-winding space deformation and barrier displacement.

4. <100mm Grounding Rule

Test lead shield grounds must be flat braided copper, strictly shorter than 100 mm, clamped direct to bare metal bushing flange to avoid artificial >300 kHz resonance nulls.
